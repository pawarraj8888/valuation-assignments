CDO Valuation with the Lintner Model

The same 1000 cases of correlated defaults as Part 1, now valued. Every tranche's cash flows are discounted at the risk-free rate in each case, the bonds' price at their 9% market yield gives the cost of risk, and each tranche is charged for risk in proportion to the covariance of its PV with the PV of the whole pool (Lecture 6).

Values

Value = expected PV at the risk-free rate less the cost per unit risk times the tranche's covariance with the pool. The three values always add up to the bonds' market value.

Bonus: choosing Class B

The bank's gain is the cash from selling both classes at par plus the residual's value, less the bonds it puts in. With Class B at its fair coupon the gain is the same at every size, so the structure moves risk between the bank and the Class B investors without creating value.
